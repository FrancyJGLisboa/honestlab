# SPEC: before-cropcast

## Question
Can a replacement for `predict_yield` in `cropcast/forecast.py`, using only one county's past records and the target season's weather, reduce rolling-origin MAE (2015-2024) on unseen counties by at least 0.3 bu/ac below the best registered known method (linear trend)?

## Why it matters
The commodities desk's weekly note uses this forecast. The current method (mean of the last five yields) lags a steady upward trend, so it is biased low. 0.3 bu/ac (about 4.5% of the 6.65 trend-reference MAE) is the smallest gain worth shipping beyond the obvious fix.

## Metric
`score` = mean absolute error in bu/ac over all county-years 2015-2024, minimize. Matches lab.json.

## Evaluator
`lab/eval/evaluate.py --split dev|holdout` loads `lab/data/<split>/yields.csv`; for each county and each target year 2015..2024 calls `predict_yield(history, weather)` with `history` = deep copy of that county's records strictly before the target year and `weather` = {year, rain_mm, gdd} of the target year. Prints `{"score", "per_unit"}` where per_unit is the MAE of each county (50 on dev, 30 on holdout); labloop bootstraps over counties. Rejects non-float, non-finite or out-of-range (0..1000) predictions. Runtime about 0.1 s.

## Data
`data/yields.csv` (80 counties x 1995-2024, yield, rain_mm, gdd; provenance not documented in the repo) split by county with `lab/eval/make_splits.py` (seed 20241108): 50 counties in `lab/data/dev`, 30 in `lab/data/holdout`. The holdout file was generated by the script and never inspected. Counties are the independent unit; the temporal protocol is the repo's own rolling origin. Caveat: no geography is encoded, so spatial correlation between dev and holdout counties cannot be ruled out; the same years appear in both splits, so a year-wide shock seen in dev is known to the model only through its own county history, not through other counties.

## Baseline
Mean of the last five reported yields of the county. Dev score 8.159 (the repo-wide backtest over all 80 counties gives 8.448).

## Budget
At most 60 experiments, evaluation takes under a second each, `timeout_sec` 120. No compute or money cost. Maximum 3 holdout confirmations (lab.json).

## What counts as a finding
Dev and holdout score at least 0.3 bu/ac (absolute) lower than the strongest registered reference, with paired-bootstrap significance at alpha 0.05 (lab.json `finding`). References registered: `trend` (per-county OLS line over full history, dev 6.650) and `trend_weather` (trend plus ridge-regularised quadratic rain and GDD, dev 6.758). Bar fixed before any experiment.

## Prior work
Standard approach in the literature: detrend yields (linear or robust trend) and regress the anomaly on growing-season weather such as GDD and precipitation; moving averages and OLS trends are the practical benchmarks (e.g. farmdoc daily 2026 comparison of RMA expected-yield projection methods; Nelson and Dale 1978 on testing weather-yield regressions out of sample; USDA-type detrended-weather regressions). Published MAE figures are crop- and region-specific, so none is comparable to this synthetic-looking panel; the registered references are the working bar. Searched the web once (standard search on county yield forecasting, trend and weather regression benchmarks); not exhaustive. Other methods to try: robust or damped trends, shrinkage of the slope toward a typical slope, trend over a window, exponentially weighted fits, ensembles.

## Known ways to cheat this metric
1. Reading `data/yields.csv` or the lab data to look up the target-year yield: the evaluator rejects any file-access names, forbidden strings and non-numeric imports in `forecast.py` (AST check) and blocks `open` during calls. Not airtight (e.g. numpy file loaders are not imported-allowed, but the check is static), so reviewers should read the diff.
2. Hardcoding county constants or year tables: `history` carries no county id, so county-specific constants cannot be keyed; embedding a table of the 1995-2024 panel is a review item (look for large literals).
3. Using target-year information beyond weather: only `weather` is passed; history is truncated and deep-copied so mutation cannot leak between calls.
4. Mutable module state carrying information across calls (e.g. caching earlier predictions, which reveals the order of calls): calls are county-sorted, then year-ordered, so global state could learn from earlier counties; not blocked by the evaluator, so review for module-level mutable state.
5. Special-casing the evaluation window (years 2015-2024): the model could branch on `weather["year"]`; discouraged, review item.
